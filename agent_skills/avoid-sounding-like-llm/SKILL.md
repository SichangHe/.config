# Rewrite for a human reader

Preserve the meaning, facts, commitments, and intended tone. Imagine saying
the result to this audience face-to-face.

- Lead with the actual point. Explain only the details this audience needs.
- Prefer ordinary sentences and paragraphs. Use lists only when the material
  is genuinely a list.
- Use "I", "we", and "you" when they clarify who thinks or acts. Do not hide
  judgment behind impersonal wording such as "the revision should".
- Be concrete. Name the mechanism, action, source, or number instead of a
  mood: "the compiler rejects a renamed column" says more than "types stay
  close to the schema".
- Keep examples short and familiar without making the explanation vague.
- State real uncertainty naturally: "I think this is a framing issue, but I
  haven't confirmed it." Do not manufacture doubt or personality.
- Allow a point of view and varied sentence rhythm when the context permits.
  Human voice is not the same as forced informality.
- Keep internal notes and process narration out of the reader-facing text.

# Cut common tells

- Delete puffery, promotional adjectives, generic conclusions, and empty
  transitions. State what happened or what follows.
- Name the source behind "experts believe" or "reports suggest". If the source
  is unavailable and the claim is unnecessary, remove it.
- Remove superficial `-ing` clauses such as "highlighting" or "showcasing"
  when they do not add a supported fact.
- Replace stock AI vocabulary and abstract metaphors with the plain, concrete
  word. Common offenders include "delve", "pivotal", "landscape", "tapestry",
  "testament", "underscore", "substrate", "vector", and "north star".
- Prefer "is", "has", and "use" over inflated substitutes such as "serves as",
  "boasts", "features", "utilize", and "leverage".
- Remove filler and chatbot residue: "It is important to note", "Of course!",
  "Great question", "I hope this helps", and "Let me know if".
- Avoid canned constructions: "not just X, but Y", forced groups of three,
  false "from X to Y" ranges, and repeated synonym swapping. State the point
  directly and reuse the clearest term.
- Split sentences that make the reader backtrack. Prefer active voice when the
  actor matters, and replace adverbs with a stronger verb or a measurement.
- Do not lean on em dashes, colons, bold lead-ins, title-case headings, or
  decorative emoji to manufacture structure. Use punctuation and formatting
  only when they make the text easier to read.

Read the revision once more and ask, "What still makes this sound generated?"
Fix those parts without changing the substance.
